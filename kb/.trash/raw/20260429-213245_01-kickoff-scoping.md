Hi everyone,

Thanks for taking the time to sync briefly earlier this week. Sending this note to get the initiative on paper, align on scope, and propose a working cadence before we go too far in any one direction.

**Background.** As most of you know, David and the MarTech leadership team committed to the Q2 roadmap item of "bringing credit card transaction data into AEP" as a foundational capability for the 2026 personalization roadmap. Today, our marketing audiences for card customers are built in the enterprise data warehouse (Snowflake) and pushed into AEP as pre-computed segment flags. That model has served us well for the last two years, but it's hitting its limits: every new audience is a new Snowflake ask, turnaround is 2–3 weeks, and we can't act on behavioral signals with anything close to the latency our business is asking for. Greg's team has been waiting on a "spent $X at dining in the last 30 days" audience since January.

The ambition of Flywheel is to flip that: put transaction-level (or near-transaction-level) signal into AEP once, governed properly, and let marketing build audiences and journeys on top of it without having to route every request back through Data Eng.

**What I'd like us to align on over the next 2–3 weeks:**

1. **Use cases in scope for MVP.** I don't want us to solve for every possible marketing use case on day one. I'm proposing we pick 3 high-confidence use cases, build for those, and use them as forcing functions for the data model and architecture. Initial candidates (from conversations with Greg and Rachel): category-based offers, dormant card reactivation, and spend-signal cross-sell. Open to debate.
2. **Grain of data.** The biggest open question. Do we ingest raw authorized transactions into AEP, or do we ingest pre-computed features (e.g., "dining spend last 30d," "dormant flag")? There are real trade-offs — cost, flexibility, governance — and I want us to land on a defensible answer, not drift into one.
3. **Latency.** Daily batch is where we are today. Streaming is technically possible. The question is whether any of our MVP use cases actually need intraday signal, or whether we're paying for real-time we won't use.
4. **Historical depth.** 13 months has been our default for a lot of data products. Is that the right number here? What do we lose at 13 vs. 24?
5. **Ownership.** Once this is live, who owns the pipeline, the schema, and the audience definitions? We should not leave this fuzzy.

Will,

Great writeup, and timely. Card LT has been pushing hard on personalization ROI numbers for the 2026 plan, and the fact that we are flying blind on behavioral marketing for card is something I've had to apologize for in two separate reviews this quarter.

A few things before Tuesday:

**On use cases.** Strong agree on your three candidates, but I'd challenge "cross-sell" being scoped broadly. If we mean "spend signals -> HELOC / auto / mortgage leads," that is going to drag Consumer Lending into the conversation and they are not staffed to integrate right now. If we mean "card -> other card products" (e.g., upgrading a classic cardholder to a rewards card based on spend), that's inside the Card org and we can move fast. I'd rather we scope cross-sell narrowly for MVP and expand later.

**On grain.** I will say publicly what I've said in 1:1s: I do not believe Card's use cases need raw transactions in AEP on day one. 80%+ of what we want to do is answered by aggregated features — spend by category, spend velocity, dormant flags, average ticket. If "raw transactions in AEP" is the thing that doubles the cost or timeline, we should think hard. That said — I'm a team player, and if Analytics has a strong argument for raw, I want to hear it.

**On merchant category.** This is the landmine. MCC codes are garbage for marketing purposes — "5411 Grocery Stores" includes Walmart, Whole Foods, and a bodega in the same bucket. Linh's team has been working on an internal merchant taxonomy project but it's nowhere near done. We need to decide whether Flywheel depends on that project finishing or whether we ship with MCCs and accept the lossy categorization.

**On ownership.** I will flag this early: Card does not want to own a data pipeline. We are a product org. We want to consume governed data and build journeys. If the expectation is that Card owns the AEP schema for transaction data, we need to have a different conversation.

Top 6 asks that have been blocked on data:

1. Triggered offer based on category spend in the last 30 days (Greg, waiting since January)
2. "Lapsed premium" winback — premium cardholders whose spend has dropped 40%+ quarter over quarter
3. Travel-spender identification for the relaunch of the Horizon card (product marketing)
4. Dormant card reactivation — our current logic is 180 days, we think 90 is better but can't test
5. Cross-sell to Horizon card from classic cardholders with high travel spend (stays inside Card org — see above)
6. Suppression of marketing to customers who are actively disputing a charge (Greg flagged this one specifically — we've been reaching out with offers to people in the middle of fraud investigations, which is a terrible customer experience)

Happy to go deeper Tuesday.

Priya

Adding Tom Bianchi who is our AEP implementation lead — he's going to be the one actually holding the wrench for most of this, so he should be in the room from the start.

Quick thoughts before Tuesday:

**Current-state plumbing, briefly.** Authorized card transactions land in Snowflake via the nightly ETL from the TSYS mainframe feed. We get ~11M authorized transactions per day. Aggregated features (spend by category, velocity, dormancy) are computed in Snowflake and pushed to AEP as audience files via the nightly batch connector. End-to-end freshness today is ~26–30 hours from authorization to audience availability in AEP.

**Two real architectural options, plus one that people will ask about but shouldn't exist:**

- **Option A — Aggregated features into AEP (enriched batch).** Extend what we already do. Add more features, push more often. Cheapest, lowest-risk, and honestly solves most of Priya's list. Cons: every new feature is a Data Eng ticket, same problem we have today just with a wider surface area.
- **Option B — Raw transactions into AEP (daily batch).** Stream the daily Snowflake transaction table into AEP as an experience event. Lets Analytics and MarTech build any feature they want inside AEP. Much more expensive to ingest (AEP bills by event volume and by profile attribute richness), but much more flexible. This is probably where Analytics wants us to go.
- **Option C — Raw transactions into AEP (streaming / real-time).** Skip Snowflake, tap the Kafka topic off the authorization stream, land in AEP in near real-time. Technically feasible. I want to flag this as "do not do for MVP" unless we have a use case that actually needs it, which I don't think we do. The cost delta is not 2x batch, it's more like 4x, and the governance and reliability story is meaningfully harder.

I'd propose we seriously debate A vs. B and treat C as a Phase 2 conversation.

**On merchant taxonomy.** Agree with Priya — MCCs alone are not a marketing primitive. But I will push back on the idea that Flywheel should block on the taxonomy project. Taxonomy is a 2027 deliverable, maybe late 2026 if everything goes right. If Flywheel blocks on it, Flywheel ships in 2027.

**On ownership.** Data Eng can own the pipeline and the schema. We will not own the audience definitions. Those live with the consumer teams (Card, Analytics, MarTech). We need to be clear about that boundary because last time we were fuzzy about it we ended up in a 4-month fight over who owns the dormant flag logic.

I'll have a 1-pager on ingestion options for Tuesday.

Marcus
